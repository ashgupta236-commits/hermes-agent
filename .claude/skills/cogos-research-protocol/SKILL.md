# Research protocol (deep-research / decision analysis)

## Prioritise by decision value
Priority(unknown) ≈ information_gain × P(changes decision) × decision_importance / cost.
Resolve decision-changing unknowns first; stop when marginal value < cost unless exhaustive
coverage is required. Do not exhaustively research low-impact facts while a high-impact unknown
is open.

## Evidence record (every finding)
source (URL/path/tool) · acquisition time · primary/secondary/tertiary · scope/definition ·
freshness (date the fact was true) · lineage (root source if this repeats another) · what
proposition it *actually* supports (often narrower than the claim).

## Independence
Five outlets repeating one report are one root source. Prefer ≥2 independent primary roots for
any claim you will rely on at ≥0.8 confidence. The belief graph discounts shared-root evidence.

## Contradictions
Never average conflicting numbers. Record the contradiction, hypothesise the cause
(scope / definition / time period / measurement / source error / genuine), run a targeted
investigation that discriminates, and keep the uncertainty visible if unresolved.

## Hypothesis tournament
For genuinely ambiguous questions generate competing hypotheses with unique predictions and
disconfirming observations; score explanatory power and posterior; prefer investigations that
discriminate between the top two; explicitly try to falsify the leader before relying on it.
Do not invent alternatives for settled facts.

## Independent cognition (for consequential conclusions)
Spawn an independent specialist that receives the objective, constraints and raw evidence but
not the executive's conclusion; extract disagreements; investigate the material ones.

## Synthesis
Conclusion · rationale grounded in the strongest evidence · per-criterion assessment (satisfied
only where verification exists) · remaining uncertainties · what would change the conclusion ·
calibrated confidence (no false precision without a calibration basis).
